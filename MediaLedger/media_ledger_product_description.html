Media Ledger · Windows desktop app · Python

Your media folders, filed by what's in the picture.

Media Ledger looks at a photo or video, compares it with the folders you already keep, and tells you which one it belongs in — and exactly why. It runs a CLIP vision model on your own machine, so nothing leaves your computer once the model is downloaded.

What it does

Point Media Ledger at a root folder of category subfolders — Marvel, Memes, Star Trek, Screenshots, whatever you use — then pick a file. The app reads the folder names as your categories, scores the file against each one, and proposes a destination. A full diagnostic report explains the decision, so you can trust it or tune it instead of guessing.

Features

Zero-shot sorting

No training and no labeled examples. Your folder names become the categories, using OpenAI's CLIP ViT-Large/14 model.

Curated prompts

Built-in descriptions for 23 franchise and topic folders sharpen the match. Variants like Star_Wars, star-wars and Star Wars resolve to the same prompt.

Two-pass matching

If the curated pass falls below your threshold, the app retries with plain prefix-based prompts before giving up.

Filename rules

Folders defined by origin rather than content — Screenshots, Pixel, WhatsApp, Snapchat, iOS and DSLR camera, motion photos — are handled by filename patterns.

Photos and video

Reads PNG, JPG, JPEG, HEIC, MP4 and MOV. Videos are judged from a frame about 15% in; photos honor phone rotation.

Explains itself

Every result shows the strategy used, the prompt, the confidence, the top three candidates, and a clickable full-size preview.

How a decision is made

  1. ReadImage loaded, or a frame pulled from video.
  2. ScoreCLIP compares it against one prompt per content folder.
  3. RetryBelow your confidence threshold, it tries fallback prompts.
  4. Fall backStill unsure? Filename conventions get a vote.
  5. ReportYou get a target, or "Unassigned" with the reason why.

Built to be dependable

Download onceThe model (about 1.7 GB) is fetched only if a complete copy isn't already stored, then always loaded locally. A lighter ~600 MB model is a one-line switch.
Self-repairingA corrupt or partial download is detected and re-fetched automatically.
Your drive, your choiceModel storage can live on any drive, with a warning when space runs low.
Plain-English errorsLoad failures come with hints for low memory, old torch versions and missing packages.
